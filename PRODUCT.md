# StudAb27

**Personal use only.** StudAb27 supports its owner's scholarship journey, with no marketing, sales, or monetization goals. Development priorities follow personal usefulness, privacy, reliability, and manageable upkeep.

## Register

product

## Users

The project owner is the sole intended user. The app supports undergraduate engineering and computer-science scholarship preparation between schoolwork and test preparation, with a small next step rather than another long list.

The primary device is a phone, often used one-handed in a quiet room at night. Keep navigation at the bottom, forms readable, and conversation drafts safe when a tab is interrupted. Desktop is a comfortable extension of the same workspace.

## Product Purpose

Track progress on a deliberately small, user-chosen set of scholarships. Each has its own source-scoped application checklist, document requirements, dates, and private notes. There is no cap on how many scholarships can be tracked. Schola helps research requirements and missing evidence; the full catalogue is available when the user wants to add another opportunity.

## Information architecture

Tracker, Documents, and Calendar form the primary navigation on phone and desktop. Both home and /scholarships open the tracker. Add scholarship opens the full catalogue. Schola stays one tap away; source monitoring, the earlier task briefing, scenarios, and settings are secondary tools.

Every scholarship has one permanent page opening on its own checklist, with overview, dates, personal plan, and sources available as tabs. Pursuit priority is Focus, Secondary, Backup, or unset (Decide later), separate from application progress and outlook. The explicitly chosen main target appears first and is always Focus. Removing an item from the tracker keeps its notes and progress. Calendar includes tracked scholarships regardless of priority, plus earlier saved and active routes, with a clear All routes switch. Keep unconfirmed and conflicting dates off calendar days.

The Description tab explains each scholarship using existing catalogue facts and scoped sources. Catalogue links open this description; the tracker also offers a direct description link while retaining Open checklist. Each scholarship page has a Schola popup specializing in that scholarship, with its own private conversation and browser-tab draft. Its context includes the relevant owner profile and that scholarship's plan, preparation, dates, and sources. General Schola remains available for broader questions and comparisons. Research sessions preserve a specific investigation across redirections and can be reopened from Schola or the Tracker notification. Sending and retrying are explicit; closing a popup preserves drafts and queued work. Model settings and daily call limits are shared.

Schola's primary role is an evidence-seeking consultant. It explores public scholarship sources through iterative search, official-page navigation and text PDF reading, then explains the findings, personal implications and unresolved evidence. Research replies and their sources persist in the relevant conversation. Source monitoring remains a separate background service. When enabled, Schola opens or continues a research session for a potentially material monitored change on a tracked scholarship. The notification links to the investigation and can be marked read independently of the finding. Schola does not choose scholarships, manage the whole journey autonomously, or turn a model answer into a verified rule.

Explore and general Schola offer Add new scholarship. An explicit public research request creates a durable local draft. Review funding, requirements, sources, unresolved questions and possible duplicates, edit the draft, then explicitly Add to Explore. Accepted additions remain in the local workspace and its export/recovery; publication to the versioned public catalogue requires separate review. Adding an entry never tracks it, sets priority, creates confirmed dates or verifies eligibility. Draft research does not read owner profile or conversation memory.

Documents opens on the document steps for tracked scholarships and retains the reusable packs in a second view. A shared document being ready never completes an institution-specific requirement automatically. Every guide step identifies its sources and whether it needs rechecking for the target cycle; completion is preparation progress, not eligibility verification. New tracked scholarships without a researched guide say so plainly. Checklists, notes, and priority choices are private and change only through explicit actions.

Scholarships can be filtered by Realistic, Reach, Lottery, or Unknown, with a visible explanation and source scope for each assessment. Judge the whole path to receiving the named award, including required admission. Automatic funding after admission does not establish a realistic overall chance. These qualitative planning categories are separate from personal priority, eligibility checks, and budget adequacy. Only matched scholarship-recipient and applicant figures may produce a published award rate; admissions rates, finalist ratios, funding coverage, and SAT targets must never become personal scholarship probabilities. Keep unsupported options Unknown, including when that leaves Realistic empty.

## Brand Personality

Professional, concise, and factual. Preserve the dark, restrained interface. Use descriptive headings, specific instructions, and neutral status messages. Keep interface copy objective: name the page, action, or state without addressing the reader as "you" or "your." Omit self-referential workspace, privacy, and hosting taglines. Keep practical privacy disclosures and evidence limits beside the relevant action. Avoid motivational slogans, forced optimism, coaching language, and patronizing reassurance. Urgency must come from published dates and explicit requirements.

## Anti-references

Endless feeds, leaderboard motivation, loud control rooms, decorative metric dashboards, and unsupported promises of eligibility. Avoid a cold terminal aesthetic and a dense spreadsheet as the default home screen.

## Design Principles

- One next step, with a small choice of alternatives.
- Funding survivability before prestige.
- Evidence and uncertainty are visible beside a claim.
- User decisions are explicit and reversible.
- Private by default; a fictional demo remains useful to public visitors.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG AA: keyboard navigation, semantic controls, visible focus, at least 4.5:1 body-text contrast, reduced motion, clear labels, and mobile layouts. Do not rely on color alone for status. These are implementation requirements, not a certification claim.
